Our Mission

Aging is marked by a gradual decline in tissue function, regenerative capacity, and cellular identity. Yet across mammals, there is extraordinary variation in lifespan and resistance to age-related degeneration — suggesting that the molecular determinants of longevity are plastic, regulated, and in some cases, reversible.

Our lab aims to uncover how regulatory elements and networks govern the aging process, maintain tissue identity, and constrain or extend organismal lifespan. By combining comparative genomics, functional epigenetics, and rejuvenation strategies, we seek to understand — and eventually reverse — the molecular hallmarks of aging.

Research Directions

1. Mechanisms of Cellular Identity Shifts During Aging

Aging is accompanied by a progressive, systemic shift in cellular identity across tissues and cell types — a phenomenon we term mesenchymal drift (MD): a pervasive upregulation of mesenchymal gene programs in epithelial, endothelial, immune, and glial cells. MD increases with disease progression and is associated with reduced survival.

We are investigating how systemic circulating factors influence cell-state transitions and identifying the cell-intrinsic pathways that mediate their effects. We aim to identify and validate therapeutic strategies — gene therapies and small-molecule modulators — to reverse mesenchymal drift and restore youthful cellular identities.

2. Enhancing Cellular Resilience to Preserve Tissue Health

Certain species and cell types possess greater intrinsic plasticity and resistance to identity loss. These interspecies differences offer a unique opportunity to uncover key molecular regulators of cellular resilience.

Leveraging comparative genomics and reprogramming biology, we aim to define the genetic and epigenetic factors that maintain cellular fidelity under stress and during aging — informing strategies to bolster cell identity and function for long-term tissue health and regenerative potential.

3. Longevity Networks Across Mammals

By analyzing transcriptomic and proteomic data from dozens of mammalian species with diverse lifespans, we identify genes whose expression scales with species’ maximum lifespan. Our work has uncovered circadian and pluripotency networks as two central pillars of longevity regulation, providing targets for anti-aging interventions across mammals.
